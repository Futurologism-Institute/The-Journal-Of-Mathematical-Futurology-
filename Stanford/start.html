**Yes.** Here is a compact, invented formal system called **MCB-Logic** (Mechano-Chemo-Bio Logic). It is a many-sorted first-order logic extended with process, conservation, and hierarchical operators designed specifically for mechanical, chemical, and bioengineering reasoning. The goal is a single symbolic language that can express structure, flow, transformation, constraint, and emergence across the three domains while remaining precise enough for formal manipulation. ### 1. Signature (Alphabet) **Sorts (types)** - \(\mathsf{Obj}\) — physical objects / components - \(\mathsf{State}\) — instantaneous configurations - \(\mathsf{Flow}\) — mass, energy, momentum, information, or signal streams - \(\mathsf{Proc}\) — processes / transformations - \(\mathsf{Const}\) — constraints / invariants - \(\mathsf{Scale}\) — hierarchical levels (molecular, continuum, system, organism…) **Individual constants & function symbols** (examples) - Mechanical: \(F, \tau, \sigma, \varepsilon, m, I, k, c\) - Chemical: \(C_i, r, K, \Delta G, \nu\) (stoichiometric coefficients) - Biological: \(\mathsf{gene}, \mathsf{prot}, \mathsf{cell}, \mathsf{tissue}, \mu, \lambda\) (growth rates) - Cross-domain: \(\mathsf{vol}, \mathsf{area}, T, P, S\) (entropy) **Predicate symbols** - \( \mathsf{Connected}(x,y) \) - \( \mathsf{Conserves}(\varphi, P) \) — quantity \(\varphi\) is conserved by process \(P\) - \( \mathsf{Transforms}(P, \alpha, \beta) \) — process \(P\) maps state/flow \(\alpha\) into \(\beta\) - \( \mathsf{Stable}(s) \), \( \mathsf{Oscillates}(s) \), \( \mathsf{Bifurcates}(s) \) - \( \mathsf{Compatible}(M, C) \) — material/structure \(M\) satisfies constraint \(C\) **Special operators** - \( \oplus \) — parallel composition of processes or components - \( ; \) — sequential composition - \( \triangleright \) — hierarchical embedding (“is realized at lower scale as”) - \( \models_{\mathsf{mech}}, \models_{\mathsf{chem}}, \models_{\mathsf{bio}} \) — domain satisfaction relations - \( \partial \) — boundary / interface operator - \( \nabla \cdot \) — divergence (used both continuously and symbolically for balance) ### 2. Core Syntax (well-formed formulas) Atomic formulas are the usual first-order atoms over the signature above. Compound formulas are built with the classical connectives \(\land, \lor, \to, \neg, \forall, \exists\) plus the engineering operators: \[ \begin{align*} P &\oplus Q &&\text{(concurrent processes)}\\ P &; Q &&\text{(sequential)}\\ \alpha &\triangleright \beta &&\text{(scale embedding)}\\ \nabla\cdot\varphi &\approx 0 &&\text{(local conservation statement)}\\ \partial P &\models_{\mathsf{d}} C &&\text{(boundary of }P\text{ satisfies constraint }C\text{ in domain }d\text{)} \end{align*} \] ### 3. Semantic Intuition (domain-specific readings) - **Mechanical** A rigid-body mechanism is an \(\mathsf{Obj}\) whose \(\mathsf{State}\) evolves under force/torque \(\mathsf{Flow}\)s subject to kinematic \(\mathsf{Const}\)s. Newton’s second law appears as the axiom schema \[ \forall m\forall a\;\mathsf{Transforms}(\mathsf{Newton}, F, m\cdot a) \] - **Chemical** A reaction network is a \(\mathsf{Proc}\) that \(\mathsf{Transforms}\) concentration vectors. Stoichiometry and mass-action kinetics are encoded by \[ \mathsf{Conserves}(\textstyle\sum_i\nu_i C_i,\; r) \land \mathsf{Transforms}(r, C, C+\nu\cdot\xi) \] - **Bioengineering** A genetic circuit or tissue construct is a hierarchical object: \[ \mathsf{tissue} \triangleright (\mathsf{cell}_1 \oplus \mathsf{cell}_2 \oplus \dots) \triangleright \mathsf{gene\text{-}network} \] Stability, homeostasis, and growth appear as temporal predicates over \(\mathsf{State}\). ### 4. Characteristic Axiom Schemata 1. **Global Conservation** \[ \mathsf{Closed}(S) \to \mathsf{Conserves}(\varphi, S) \quad\text{for mass, energy, momentum, charge, \ldots} \] 2. **Interface Compatibility** \[ \partial P_1 = \partial P_2 \to \mathsf{Compatible}(P_1,P_2) \] 3. **Scale Consistency** \[ (\alpha \triangleright \beta) \land \mathsf{Transforms}(P,\beta,\beta') \to \exists Q\;(\alpha \triangleright \beta' \land \mathsf{Transforms}(Q,\alpha,\alpha')) \] 4. **Thermodynamic Arrow** (shared by all three domains) \[ \mathsf{Transforms}(P,s,s') \to S(s') \ge S(s) \] (entropy non-decrease for isolated systems). ### 5. Illustrative Expressions **Mechanical — static equilibrium of a truss** \[ \forall e\in\mathsf{Edges}\;\mathsf{Transforms}(\mathsf{static},\;F_e,\;0) \land \mathsf{Compatible}(\mathsf{geometry},\;\mathsf{compatibility}) \] **Chemical — continuous stirred-tank reactor** \[ \nabla\cdot(v C) = r(C) \land \mathsf{Conserves}(\textstyle\sum C_i,\;\mathsf{CSTR}) \] **Bioengineering — engineered tissue construct** \[ \mathsf{scaffold} \oplus \mathsf{cells} \;\triangleright\; \mathsf{tissue} \land \mathsf{Stable}(\mathsf{homeostasis}) \land \partial\mathsf{tissue}\models_{\mathsf{bio}}\mathsf{immune\text{-}compatible} \] **Cross-domain — drug-eluting stent** \[ (\mathsf{mech\text{-}structure} \oplus \mathsf{chem\text{-}release}) \;\triangleright\; \mathsf{bio\text{-}response} \land \mathsf{Conserves}(\mathsf{drug\text{-}mass},\;\mathsf{release}) \] ### 6. Why this is useful - A single formal language lets one write, and in principle machine-check, statements that mix continuum mechanics, reaction networks, and living systems. - The operators \(\oplus, ;, \triangleright\) give a process-algebra flavour familiar to systems engineers while remaining first-order. - Conservation and thermodynamic schemas act as soundness filters: any derivation that violates them is immediately rejected. - The system is deliberately open-ended; new predicates (fatigue, mutation, catalysis, \ldots) can be added without breaking the core.
