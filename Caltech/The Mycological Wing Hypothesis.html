Abstract This paper proposes, with absolutely no scientific credibility whatsoever, the Mycological Wing Hypothesis (MWH): the proposition that near lethal doses of magic mushrooms might cause members of Al-Qaeda, ISWAP, Boko Haram, JNIM, Hamas, to develop wings, thereby improving their logistical capabilities, aerial mobility, and business performance. The hypothesis is, of course, biologically impossible. Evolutionary biology provides no mechanism by which ingesting psilocybin could cause an individual human to suddenly develop functional wings. More importantly, an individual’s acquired anatomical characteristics generally cannot simply be transmitted to offspring. Evolution requires heritable variation, differential reproductive success, and changes in population frequencies across generations. Nevertheless, the hypothesis provides an amusing framework for examining several genuine concepts in evolutionary biology—including mutation, natural selection, adaptation, phenotypic plasticity, convergent evolution, and evolutionary timescales. 1. The Central Hypothesis Let a population be represented by P={x1,x2,…,xn} and let each organism possess a phenotype ϕ(xi). The Mycological Wing Hypothesis proposes the fictional transformation Human→MushroomWinged Human. Unfortunately for the hypothesis, biology responds: P(functional wings∣psilocybin)≈0. The mushrooms have therefore failed their first performance review. 2. Why Evolution Does Not Work This Way A central misconception underlying the fictional proposal is the idea that organisms can intentionally acquire useful characteristics during their lifetimes and then transmit those characteristics genetically. Suppose an individual takes a psychedelic substance and subsequently develops an imaginary pair of wings. Even granting the impossible phenotype, ϕadult→wings, there is no automatic mechanism ϕadult→Goffspring. Evolution instead requires genetic variation: G1,G2,…,Gn followed by differential reproductive success. A simplified evolutionary model is pt+1=ptwAptwA+(1−pt)wB, where pt represents the frequency of an allele or trait and wA,wB represent relative fitness. Thus, if wing development were somehow genetically determined, winged individuals would need to reproduce at different rates for natural selection to alter the population. Taking mushrooms is not a substitute for heritability. 3. The Business Case for Wings The fictional consultants behind MWH nevertheless identify several supposed commercial advantages.
